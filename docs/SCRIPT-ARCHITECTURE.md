# Script Architecture — Investigative Disaster Documentary

Extracted from five transcripts of a single channel (Katrina 2005, Carr Fire tornado
2018, Harvey 2017, Mount St. Helens 1980, Picher tornado 2008). Every beat below
appears in at least 4 of the 5; the ones marked **[5/5]** appear in all of them.

This is the *structural* template. It carries no content from the source
channel — only the shape. Content comes from `FACT-SLOTS.md`.

---

## The thesis engine

One idea drives this entire format:

> **The disaster is not the disaster.** The natural event is a trigger. The real
> subject is an institutional failure that was documented in advance and ignored.

| Episode | Trigger | Actual subject |
|---|---|---|
| Katrina | Category 3 landfall | 40 years of known levee defects |
| Mount St. Helens | Lateral blast | A restricted zone drawn by politics, not science |
| Picher | EF4 tornado | 50 years of unremediated toxic waste |
| Harvey | Stalled cyclone | Category rating misrepresenting real risk |
| Carr Fire | Fire tornado | A phenomenon no warning system existed for |

**If a candidate topic has no institutional-failure thesis, it does not fit this
format.** That is the primary gate in the ideation pipeline.

---

## Beat sheet — 25-minute target (~3,750 words at 150 wpm)

| # | Beat | Words | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold open **[5/5]** | 60–90 | Stakes inside 5 seconds |
| 2 | Archival audio insert **[5/5]** | 40–80 | Voice change resets attention |
| 3 | Stat burst | 20–30 | Scale, in fragments |
| 4 | **Thesis turn [5/5]** | 80–120 | Opens the loop that carries the episode |
| 5 | "And everyone knew" | 250–350 | Documented foreknowledge, ignored |
| 6 | Signposted background | 400–550 | Geography / history / mechanism |
| 7 | Timestamped escalation | 900–1200 | The spine |
| 8 | Named victim stories ×3–4 | 600–900 | Emotional peaks |
| 9 | Forward-pull closers | ~100 | Anti-drop-off, woven between sections |
| 10 | Aftermath numbers | 250–350 | Authority through specificity |
| 11 | Institutional reckoning | 300–400 | What officially changed |
| 12 | **Epigram [5/5]** | 20–40 | The quotable inversion |
| 13 | Outro **[5/5]** | 100–130 | Like/sub ask, after value |

For a 30-minute episode add one victim story (+250) and extend beats 6 and 7.
Do not pad beats 1–4; the opening is fixed-length by design.

---

## Beat specifications

### 1 — Cold open `[5/5]`

```
{MONTH} {DAY}, {YEAR}. {TIME_OF_DAY} in {PLACE}.
{ONE_SENTENCE_SITUATION, present tense}
```

Observed openings, all five:
- *"August 29th, 2005. It's early morning on the Gulf Coast, and one of the most powerful hurricanes in American history is bearing down…"*
- *"July 26th, 2018. A tornado just formed inside a wildfire."*
- *"May 18th, 1980. 8:32 on a Sunday morning in Southwest Washington."*

**Rules:** exact date first, always. Present tense. No channel branding, no
"welcome back," no logo. The Carr Fire open is the strongest — nine words that
state an impossibility.

### 2 — Archival audio insert `[5/5]`

Real recorded audio within the first ~60 seconds, marked `>>` in transcript.
Sourced from: 911 calls, live news broadcasts, official press conferences,
storm-chaser footage.

**Function is mechanical, not decorative:** a different human voice at ~45s
interrupts the narration cadence right where first-drop-off clusters.

### 3 — Stat burst

Fragments, no verbs, escalating magnitude:
> *"143 mph. 2,700° F, 18,000 ft tall."*

### 4 — Thesis turn `[5/5]` — the most important beat

```
But {EVENT} wasn't the only threat.
{TRUE_CAUSE} had been {FAILING_VERB} for {DURATION}.
And {WHO_KNEW} knew it.
```

Observed:
- *"Because the storm wasn't the only threat. What destroyed the city had been quietly failing for 40 years. And every single engineer who looked at it knew it."*
- *"But the tornado wasn't the beginning of Pitcher's disaster. In fact, the town had been dying for decades."*

This is the open loop. Everything after it is the payoff. Land this wrong and
retention collapses at minute three.

### 5 — "And everyone knew"

Escalating evidence of ignored warnings. Always includes at least one **dated,
named document or study**, and where available a quote showing dismissal:

> *"there were a lot of federal folks who were just in the back room laughing"*

### 6 — Signposted background

```
To understand {EVENT}, you first have to understand {SUBJECT}.
```
4/5 use this construction almost verbatim. Then: plain-language geography,
history, or physical mechanism. No jargon without immediate translation.

### 7 — Timestamped escalation

The spine. Precise clock times as de facto section headers:
> *"At 6:10 a.m. on August 29th, 2005…"* · *"At 8:32:11 a.m…."* · *"At 5:39 p.m. Central Daylight Time…"*

**Present tense throughout.** Each timestamp is a micro-progress marker — the
viewer feels forward motion, which is what holds a 25-minute runtime.

### 8 — Named victim stories ×3–4

```
{FULL_NAME}, {AGE}, {ROLE_OR_RELATIONSHIP}, {ONE_HUMANISING_DETAIL}.
{TERSE_OUTCOME_SENTENCE}
```

The outcome line is short, flat, and unadorned. This restraint is the whole
effect:
- *"She never returned."*
- *"They had. He had not."*
- *"He stays with his 16 cats."*
- *"He spends his last minute on the film instead of himself."*

**Never** editorialise the outcome. No "tragically," no "heartbreakingly."

Space these evenly through beat 7 — they are the attention resets.

### 9 — Forward-pull closers

One sentence at each section boundary, pointing forward:
- *"And what was coming for them was far worse than they could imagine."*
- *"All it needed was a spark."*
- *"But something else was beginning to happen above the fire."*
- *"And the storm was just getting started."*

### 10 — Aftermath numbers

Death toll · damage in dollars · structures destroyed · population change ·
area affected. Specific figures only, with the revising body named where the
number has been revised.

### 11 — Institutional reckoning

What officially changed: the admission, the study, the new agency, the retired
name, the money spent. Where the reckoning is inadequate, say so plainly and
let a quote carry it:

> *"Do I feel safe? … No."*

### 12 — Epigram `[5/5]` — the signature

An **antithesis that inverts the premise**, one or two sentences:
- *"The storm didn't destroy New Orleans, the system did."*
- *"The storm didn't destroy Pitcher. It just made sure that everyone could see what was already there."*
- *"In 1980, the maps drew a circle around the summit and called everything outside it safe. The mountain exploded sideways."*
- *"Firefighters know how to fight fire. They know how to read wind. But nobody taught them how to survive a tornado made of both."*

Pattern: `Not {ASSUMED_CAUSE} — {ACTUAL_CAUSE}.` This is the most-quoted,
most-screenshotted line. It is what gets the video sent to someone.

### 13 — Outro `[5/5]`

```
If you made it this far, {ACKNOWLEDGEMENT}.
These {stories/videos} take {EFFORT}, and every {like, comment, subscription} {HELPS}.
{THANKS_TO_EXISTING_AUDIENCE}
{SIGN_OFF} and I'll see you in the next one.
```
Observed sign-offs: *"Stay safe"*, *"Stay alert"*. Two episodes open the outro
with: *"Every storm eventually ends, but the way we hold each other, that remains."*

## Retention mechanics — why each beat earns its place

| Beat | Algorithmic function |
|---|---|
| 1 | Clears the 30-second cliff — stakes before any branding |
| 2 | Voice change at ~45s, where first drop-off clusters |
| 4 | The open loop; single largest driver of full-length retention |
| 7 | Timestamps as progress markers — perceived forward motion |
| 8 | Emotional peaks spaced to reset attention decay |
| 9 | Explicit anti-drop-off sentences at every section seam |
| 12 | The quotable line — drives comments and DM sends |
| 13 | Ask placed *after* value delivered, never before |

**Watch time is the ranking signal this format optimises**, and a 25-minute video
retained at 50% beats a 10-minute video retained at 60%. That is the whole reason
for the length.

---

## Invariants

1. **Tense discipline.** Present tense for the event timeline; past for background and aftermath. Never mix within a paragraph.
2. **Short sentences. Deliberate fragments.** *"Some made it, some did not."*
3. **No editorialising on death.** The flat outcome line does the work.
4. **Every number is specific and attributed.** No "thousands" where a figure exists.
5. **No second person except in beat 6's signpost.**
6. **The epigram must invert, not summarise.**
7. **No cold-open branding.** Ever.

---

## What this template does *not* license

The structure is reusable. The content is not. Beats 5, 7, 8, 10 and 11 assert
verifiable facts about real people and real institutions — see `FACT-SLOTS.md`.
Generating those from a language model, rather than extracting them from sources,
is how this format produces defamation instead of documentary.
